# definition def:rigorous-interpretation

## statement

Let \((X(t))_{t\geq 0}\) be the state process of the mass-action-kinetics
chemical reaction network, on a probability space
\((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\), and let
\[
  \mathcal F_t:=\sigma\{X(s):0\leq s\leq t\}
\]
(completed in the usual way) be its natural filtration.  The assumed Markov
property is with respect to this filtration.  Let
\(0\leq t_1<\cdots<t_n\), put \(t_0=0\), and regard the observed values
\(y_1,\ldots,y_n\) as fixed.  Write
\[
  \ell_i(x):=L(y_i\mid x).
\]
Fix one \(k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), and set
\[
  Z_k:=g(X(t_n))\prod_{i=k+1}^{n}\ell_i(X(t_i)).                 \tag{1}
\]
The expression \(\mathbb E[Z_k\mid X(t)]\) means
\(\mathbb E[Z_k\mid\sigma(X(t))]\).  The martingale asserted in the
problem is the process
\[
  M_t^{(k)}:=\mathbb E[Z_k\mid X(t)],\qquad t_k\leq t\leq t_{k+1}, \tag{2}
\]
relative to the restricted natural filtration
\((\mathcal F_t)_{t_k\leq t\leq t_{k+1}}\).  We make the standard necessary
integrability assumption
\[
  \mathbb E\lvert g(X(t_n))\rvert<\infty.                         \tag{3}
\]

This fixed-\(k\), future-window reading is essential: a martingale is indexed
by \(t\), while the terminal random variable \(Z_k\) is held fixed.

## proof

This paragraph fixes notation and makes explicit the filtration, time range,
and integrability convention implicit in the informal statement.  No
mathematical assertion beyond those conventions is being proved here.

# lemma lem:integrability

## statement

Under the conventions of Definition `def:rigorous-interpretation`, the random
variable \(Z_k\) in (1) is integrable.

## proof

For a Gaussian observation model with nondegenerate covariance matrix
\(\Sigma_i\) in observation dimension \(d_i\),
\[
  \ell_i(x)
  =\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d_i/2}(\det\Sigma_i)^{1/2}}
    \exp\!\left[-\frac12(y_i-x)^{\mathsf T}
                       \Sigma_i^{-1}(y_i-x)\right].
\]
Thus \(0<\ell_i(x)\leq C_i\), where
\(C_i=(2\pi)^{-d_i/2}(\det\Sigma_i)^{-1/2}<\infty\).  (If the symbol
\(\sigma\) in the problem denotes a scalar variance or standard deviation,
this is the corresponding one-dimensional formula.)  Consequently,
\[
  |Z_k|
  \leq \left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{n}C_i\right)|g(X(t_n))|.
\]
The right-hand side is integrable by (3), so \(Z_k\in L^1(\mathbb P)\).

# lemma lem:future-markov-reduction

## statement

Let \(u_1\leq\cdots\leq u_m\) and let \(t\leq u_1\).  If
\(\Phi(X(u_1),\ldots,X(u_m))\) is integrable, then for the Markov process
\(X\) there is a measurable function \(H_t\) such that
\[
 \mathbb E[\Phi(X(u_1),\ldots,X(u_m))\mid\mathcal F_t]
 =H_t(X(t))
 =\mathbb E[\Phi(X(u_1),\ldots,X(u_m))\mid X(t)]                 \tag{4}
\]
almost surely.

## proof

The state space of a chemical reaction network is a countable subset of
\(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^d\), so its transition probabilities are ordinary
kernels.  Write \(P_{r,s}(x,dx')\) for the transition kernel from time \(r\)
to time \(s\).  For bounded \(\Phi\), define
\[
 H_t(x)=\int P_{t,u_1}(x,dx_1)P_{u_1,u_2}(x_1,dx_2)\cdots
                 P_{u_{m-1},u_m}(x_{m-1},dx_m)\,
                 \Phi(x_1,\ldots,x_m).                           \tag{5}
\]
Repeated application of the one-step Markov property, starting at \(u_m\)
and conditioning backwards, gives the first equality in (4).  The same
conclusion for an integrable \(\Phi\) follows by applying the bounded result
to truncations of its positive and negative parts and then using conditional
monotone convergence.

Because \(H_t(X(t))\) is \(\sigma(X(t))\)-measurable and
\(\sigma(X(t))\subseteq\mathcal F_t\), conditioning the first equality in
(4) once more on \(\sigma(X(t))\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\Phi(X(u_1),\ldots,X(u_m))\mid X(t)]
 =\mathbb E[H_t(X(t))\mid X(t)]=H_t(X(t)),
\]
which is the second equality.

# lemma lem:doob-martingale

## statement

If \(Z\in L^1(\mathbb P)\), then
\(D_t:=\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F_t]\) is an
\((\mathcal F_t)\)-martingale.

## proof

For each \(t\), \(D_t\) is \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable and conditional
Jensen's inequality gives
\(\mathbb E|D_t|\leq\mathbb E|Z|<\infty\).  If \(s\leq t\), then
\(\mathcal F_s\subseteq\mathcal F_t\), and the tower property yields
\[
  \mathbb E[D_t\mid\mathcal F_s]
  =\mathbb E[\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F_t]\mid\mathcal F_s]
  =\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal F_s]
  =D_s.
\]
These are exactly the defining properties of a martingale.

# proposition prop:full-horizon-form

## statement

For completeness, the full-filtration martingale generated by \(Z_k\) has,
for every \(t\in[t_k,t_n]\), the state-space representation
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_k\mid\mathcal F_t]
 =A_t^{(k)}B_t^{(k)}(X(t)),                                      \tag{6}
\]
where
\[
 A_t^{(k)}:=\prod_{\substack{k+1\leq i\leq n\\ t_i\leq t}}
                    \ell_i(X(t_i)),
 \qquad
 B_t^{(k)}(X(t))
 :=\mathbb E\!\left[
       g(X(t_n))
       \prod_{\substack{k+1\leq i\leq n\\t_i>t}}
                    \ell_i(X(t_i))\,\middle|\,X(t)
   \right].                                                       \tag{7}
\]
In particular, \((A_t^{(k)}B_t^{(k)}(X(t)))_{t_k\leq t\leq t_n}\)
is a martingale.  Before the first future measurement, namely for
\(t\in[t_k,t_{k+1}]\), formula (6) reduces to the expression in (2) (with
the evident reassignment of the factor at the endpoint).

## proof

The product \(A_t^{(k)}\) consists only of observations at times at most
\(t\), so it is \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable.  Factor it out of the
conditional expectation of (1).  Every random variable remaining inside the
conditional expectation is evaluated at a time at least \(t\).  Lemma
`lem:future-markov-reduction` therefore replaces its conditional expectation
given \(\mathcal F_t\) by the conditional expectation given \(X(t)\), which
is \(B_t^{(k)}(X(t))\).  This proves (6).  Lemmas `lem:integrability` and
`lem:doob-martingale` show that its left-hand side is a martingale.

If \(t<t_{k+1}\), then \(A_t^{(k)}=1\), so (6) is exactly (2).  At
\(t=t_{k+1}\), the newly accrued factor
\(\ell_{k+1}(X(t_{k+1}))\) is itself a function of the current state, so
moving it between \(A_t^{(k)}\) and \(B_t^{(k)}\) leaves the product equal to
\(\mathbb E[Z_k\mid X(t)]\).  After \(t_{k+1}\), the accrued product need
not be determined by \(X(t)\); this is why it must remain in (6).

# theorem thm:martingale-property

## statement

X is a stochastic process associated to a MAK chemical reaction network for which the markov property holds.
Given L(y_i | X(t_i)) be a gaussian likelihood coming from the obs model y = x + eps where eps is normally distributed (N(0,sigma)). X(t_i) the state of a Chemical Reaction Network at time t_i; g a real valued output function on the same domain as X(t_i). Let 1..n be the measurement time indexes and let X(t) be known. show that E[ g(X(t_n)) prod_{i=k+1}^{n} L(y_i|X(t_i)) | X(t)] is a martingale.

Precisely, the standard filtering meaning of the preceding informal statement
is the following.  The Gaussian noise law is nondegenerate; the observations
\(y_i\) are fixed; \(k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) is fixed; the filtration is
the natural filtration \(\mathcal F_t=\sigma(X(s):s\leq t)\); and
\[
 \mathbb E\left|g(X(t_n))
       \prod_{i=k+1}^{n}L(y_i\mid X(t_i))\right|<\infty.          \tag{9}
\]
Then, for \(t\) ranging over the inter-measurement interval
\([t_k,t_{k+1}]\),
\[
 t\longmapsto
 \mathbb E\!\left[g(X(t_n))
       \prod_{i=k+1}^{n}L(y_i\mid X(t_i))\,\middle|\,X(t)\right] \tag{10}
\]
is an \((\mathcal F_t)\)-martingale.  The stronger assumption
\(\mathbb E|g(X(t_n))|<\infty\) used in Definition
`def:rigorous-interpretation` implies (9) by Lemma `lem:integrability`.

If instead a martingale on the entire interval \([t_k,t_n]\) is intended,
the correct statement is Proposition `prop:full-horizon-form`: one must use
\(\mathbb E[Z_k\mid\mathcal F_t]=A_t^{(k)}B_t^{(k)}(X(t))\), retaining
the accrued likelihood factors.  Without either this replacement or the
inter-measurement time restriction, the state-only assertion is not true in
general.

## proof

Use the precise hypotheses stated immediately after the original informal
wording.  With \(Z_k\) as in (1), assumption (9) says exactly that
\(Z_k\in L^1\).  (Under the sufficient assumption (3), the same conclusion
also follows from Lemma `lem:integrability`.)

For every \(t\in[t_k,t_{k+1}]\), all states occurring in \(Z_k\) are at
times \(t_{k+1},\ldots,t_n\), no earlier than \(t\).  Applying Lemma
`lem:future-markov-reduction` with
\[
  \Phi(x_{k+1},\ldots,x_n)
  =g(x_n)\prod_{i=k+1}^{n}\ell_i(x_i)
\]
therefore gives
\[
 M_t^{(k)}
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
      g(X(t_n))\prod_{i=k+1}^{n}L(y_i\mid X(t_i))
      \,\middle|\,X(t)
   \right]
 =\mathbb E[Z_k\mid\mathcal F_t]                                \tag{8}
\]
almost surely.  Lemma `lem:doob-martingale` applied to the fixed integrable
terminal variable \(Z_k\) now shows that the right-hand side, and hence the
displayed process, is an \((\mathcal F_t)\)-martingale.  Explicitly, whenever
\(t_k\leq s\leq t\leq t_{k+1}\),
\[
 \mathbb E[M_t^{(k)}\mid\mathcal F_s]
 =\mathbb E[\mathbb E[Z_k\mid\mathcal F_t]\mid\mathcal F_s]
 =\mathbb E[Z_k\mid\mathcal F_s]
 =M_s^{(k)},
\]
which proves the claim.
